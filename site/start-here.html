Start Here: Your First Hour

You have one evening and one free library card. Here is the exact sequence the directory is built around. Do the steps in order; each one uses a page of this site.

  1. Write down one brick-wall person. Full name, one place, one date. Not

"my grandparents' whole tree" - one person. Start with the most recent hard-to-place ancestor you know (great-grandparents are ideal).

  1. Create a free FamilySearch account and run a record search for that

person (see FamilySearch Record Search). Accept the account prompt; it unlocks images. Stop when you find one plausible record.

  1. Print or open the citing-a-source page and copy that one record onto a

five-fact card. One citation, one minute, forever findable.

  1. Check the census trail: the 1950 census at the National Archives portal,

then walk backwards family by family (Census Lookups page). Census-Hopping is how beginners first see a family actually moving.

  1. Read one newspaper page. Try Chronicling America for the person's state

and decade, or your library's Newspapers.com route. One article mentioning them (even "Mr. and Mrs. Kelly visited relatives") is gold.

  1. Request one gravestone photo on Find A Grave - politely, after reading

the etiquette page - and check whether their headstone photo already exists.

  1. Fill binder checklist items for everything found today, and write a

single "Next Step" at the top of that surname's divider.

That is a complete loop: search, cite, preserve, plan. Every following session starts with reading the Next Step on the divider - not with a blank search box.
