Offline Research Binder Checklist

The internet forgets. Pages move, paywalls go up, accounts lapse. A paper binder is the backup that never changes its URL. Copy every finding into it using this checklist - about five minutes per finding.

Set up once

  • One 3-ring binder, a pack of pocket dividers (one per surname), and

archivally-safe page protectors (regular sheet protectors are fine to start; "acid-free" ones matter for originals).

  • A "Table of Contents" page at the front - one line per finding, updated

every session.

  • A stack of printed source cards and a stack of blank research-log pages

(one log line per time you touch a record, even the dead-end ones).

For each new finding

  • Write the citation first (see the citing-a-source page), before the

facts, so "where did I see this?" is never a mystery.

  • Transcribe, do not paraphrase. Copy names, dates, and spellings

exactly as written, including "Mg." for marriage and initials.

  • Note the exact spelling quirks. If the census says "Doil" for Doyle,

write it as the enumerator did, then bracket-correct it: Doil [probably Doyle].

  • Screenshot or download the image now, file it as

SURNAME_First_event_year.jpg in a folder mirroring your binder, and note the filename on the binder page. Digital copies can go in the same three-ring world: print the picture and keep the file name written on it.

  • Source every fact once, not fifty times. A finding page says

"Source 12" instead of repeating the whole citation; Source 12 has its own card.

  • Mark the confidence level. Write a small letter next to each fact:

p for proven (documented), s for secondary (someone else's tree), g for guessed (bracketed inference).

  • Fold the dead ends into the log. Families are proven by elimination
  • "checked 1920 census, not in McLean Co." saves your future self a

wasted evening. One line each.

  • Date every page with the actual day you wrote it. Today-you and

next-month-you are different researchers.

Before you close the session

  • Update the Table of Contents.
  • Skim anything you are still unsure about and mark a single Next Step

in bold at the top of the surname's divider. One next step, not five.

  • Put the binder back on the same shelf. The best filing system is the

one you will actually re-open.

Why paper, seriously

  • A binder survives housing moves, login changes, and the site that deleted its

free images while you were not looking.

  • Paper is a forced transcription pass, which is itself a check: mistyped

families are caught the second you write them out by hand.

  • It is the copy you can bring to the county courthouse, the cemetery, or a

cousin's kitchen table, where the next record usually lives.
