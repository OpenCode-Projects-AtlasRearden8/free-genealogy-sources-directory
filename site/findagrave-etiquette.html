Find A Grave Etiquette

Find A Grave is free to use, but it is built entirely by volunteers. Treat every contributor the way you would want to be treated at a cemetery gate. These are the community norms that keep the site pleasant to work in.

Do

  • Say thank you. If a volunteer filled your photo request, leave a simple

"Thank you!" note on the memorial. It takes ten seconds and it is the entire fuel that keeps the volunteer system running.

  • Add sources when you contribute facts. If you add a burial date, say where

you found it (headstone inscription, obituary, cemetery office).

  • Link family members together. The "Add relation" links on memorials are

how volunteer work turns into a real family tree the day after you log off.

  • Use the "Suggest a correction" button. If a memorial has a wrong date,

suggest the fix instead of leaving a comment farm. Corrections keep one clean record instead of three conflicting ones.

  • Copy the memorial page itself into your notes (URL and date you viewed

it), so your binder entry stays findable if the memorial is ever edited.

Do not

  • Do not rewrite other people's memorials. If someone manages a memorial,

respect that. Duplicates and turf edits start when people "take over" a page.

  • Do not demand photos with deadlines. A photo request is a favor, not an

order. Volunteers search on their own schedule; some cemeteries need a drive of several hours.

  • Do not post information about living people. Birth dates, addresses, and

photos of the living do not belong on a memorial site. Memorialize only the deceased, and leave out the living relatives' private details.

  • Do not treat Find A Grave as a source itself. It is an *index of

photographs* of headstones. The headstone is the source. For anything critical (a birth year, a maiden name), verify against a death certificate, obituary, or church record, then cite that.

  • Do not scrape or mass-download. Large data pulls violate the site's terms

and hurt the volunteers who pay for its hosting.

A good photo request looks like this

"Hello! Would you be willing to take a photo of the headstone of John Alden Kelly (1873-1921), Find A Grave memorial #12345, at Maple Hill Cemetery, Plot 14? He is my great-great-grandfather. If it is a long drive for you, no worries at all - and thank you either way!"

Short, specific, respectful, no deadline. That one gets answered.
